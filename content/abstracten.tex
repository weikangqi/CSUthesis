\begin{abstracten}\setlength{\baselineskip}{20pt}
Multi-person 3D human pose estimation for mobile robots, intelligent surveillance, and immersive interaction requires both accurate spatial localization and real-time on-device inference. Monocular approaches suffer from depth ambiguity, while conventional binocular cascade pipelines usually introduce redundant symmetric computation, expensive dense stereo matching, and poor deployability on resource-constrained devices. Therefore, achieving accurate binocular 3D pose estimation with lightweight modeling and efficient mobile deployment remains a key challenge.

To address this problem, a lightweight binocular 3D human pose estimation framework for mobile platforms is developed. At the model level, an asymmetric dual-branch backbone with a heavy left branch and a lightweight right branch is designed to reduce redundant binocular computation. An epipolar feature enhancement module is introduced to perform cross-view interaction within a one-dimensional disparity window, avoiding explicit high-dimensional cost volumes. A differentiable weighted triangulation solver further unifies soft-argmax, confidence weighting, and binocular triangulation into an end-to-end trainable formulation, while a cross-branch consistency constraint is adopted to improve the geometric representation capability of the lightweight branch. At the deployment level, hardware-aware optimization is conducted on the Snapdragon 8 Gen3 CPU platform. Latency breakdown, Roofline analysis, and quantization sensitivity screening are used to identify bottleneck and high-risk layers, based on which a layer-wise mixed-precision scheme is designed and deployed with histogram-calibrated post-training quantization on MNN.

Experiments on the RT-Pose dataset show that the proposed method achieves an average MPJPE of 158.6 mm, outperforming both monocular baselines and cascade methods based on dense depth priors, while keeping the model lightweight with 11.70 M parameters and 13.93 G FLOPs. On a Xiaomi 14 device, the mixed-precision deployment reduces single-person latency from 116.0 ms for the FP16 baseline to 72.3 ms, achieving a 1.60$\times$ speedup, and compresses the model size from 23.4 MB to 15.2 MB with only a 2.2 mm increase in MPJPE. Multi-person experiments further verify that the proposed full-image inference pipeline preserves stable latency growth after quantization.

The results demonstrate that combining asymmetric structure design, geometry-guided cross-view feature interaction, and hardware-aware mixed-precision deployment can substantially improve mobile inference efficiency while maintaining strong 3D pose estimation accuracy. The study provides a practical reference for the design and deployment of binocular 3D perception systems on resource-constrained devices.
\end{abstracten}
