\begin{abstracten}\setlength{\baselineskip}{20pt}
Multi-person 3D human pose estimation for mobile robotics, intelligent surveillance, and immersive interaction requires both spatial localization accuracy and real-time on-device inference. Monocular methods are limited by scale and depth ambiguity, while existing stereo methods often suffer from redundant dual-branch feature extraction, dependence on dense disparity or cost-volume computation, and poor efficiency on mobile hardware. To address these issues, the main work of this thesis can be summarized in the following two aspects:

  (1) To address redundant computation and the reliance on dense disparity in mobile stereo methods, this thesis proposes a lightweight binocular 3D human pose estimation model. The model adopts an asymmetric stereo backbone to reduce redundant computation between the two branches, introduces an epipolar feature enhancement module for geometry-constrained cross-view interaction, and combines a differentiable weighted triangulation solver with a cross-branch consistency constraint for end-to-end 3D recovery. On RT-Pose, a real-world and challenging binocular benchmark, the proposed method achieves an average MPJPE of 158.6 mm, outperforming both monocular baselines and cascade methods relying on dense depth priors, while maintaining 11.70 M parameters and 13.93 G FLOPs. In the RT-Pose$\rightarrow$MADS zero-shot generalization test, the model further achieves an average MPJPE of 33.68 mm.

  (2) To address latency and quantization sensitivity during on-device deployment, this thesis further proposes a hardware-aware mixed-precision deployment scheme for target platforms. The scheme combines end-to-end latency breakdown, Roofline analysis, and module-level quantization sensitivity screening to form a layer-wise mixed-precision strategy with low-bit quantization for the main convolutional path and high precision reserved for key sensitive layers. The deployment results show that the scheme achieves a 1.60$\times$ speedup on Xiaomi 14 with only a 2.2 mm increase in MPJPE. On the Jetson AGX Xavier + TensorRT platform, the mixed-precision engine further reaches 48.5 FPS with 298 MB GPU memory usage and 0.375 J energy per frame, while incurring the same 2.2 mm MPJPE increase over FP16, indicating that the proposed method is also deployable on a representative embedded GPU platform.

\end{abstracten}
