\begin{abstracten}\setlength{\baselineskip}{20pt}
    
Multi-person 3D human pose estimation for mobile robotics, intelligent surveillance, and immersive interaction requires both spatial localization accuracy and real-time on-device inference. Monocular methods are limited by scale and depth ambiguity, while existing stereo methods often suffer from redundant dual-branch feature extraction, dependence on dense disparity or cost-volume computation, and poor efficiency on mobile hardware. To address these issues, the main work of this thesis can be summarized in the following two aspects:

  (1) To address redundant computation and the reliance on dense disparity in mobile stereo methods, this thesis proposes a lightweight binocular 3D human pose estimation model. The model adopts an asymmetric stereo backbone to reduce redundant computation between the two branches, introduces an epipolar feature enhancement module for geometry-constrained cross-view interaction, and combines a differentiable weighted triangulation solver with a cross-branch consistency constraint for end-to-end 3D recovery. On the RT-Pose dataset, the proposed method achieves an average MPJPE of 158.6 mm, outperforming both monocular baselines and cascade methods relying on dense depth priors, while maintaining 11.70 M parameters and 13.93 G FLOPs. In the RT-Pose$\rightarrow$MADS zero-shot generalization test, the model further achieves an average MPJPE of 33.68 mm.

  (2) To address latency and quantization sensitivity during on-device deployment, this thesis further proposes a hardware-aware mixed-precision deployment scheme for mobile hardware platforms. The scheme uses end-to-end latency breakdown to locate the main bottlenecks, combines Roofline analysis to distinguish compute-bound and memory-bound modules, and applies module-level quantization sensitivity screening to identify high-risk layers, based on which a layer-wise mixed-precision strategy is formed with low-bit quantization for the main convolutional path and high precision reserved for key sensitive layers. Deployment results on a Xiaomi 14 device show that the scheme reduces single-person latency from 116.0 ms to 72.3 ms, achieving a 1.60$\times$ speedup, and compresses the model size from 23.4 MB to 15.2 MB with only a 2.2 mm increase in MPJPE; latency growth in multi-person scenes also remains stable.

\end{abstracten}
